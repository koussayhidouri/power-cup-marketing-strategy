# 04. Market Research

## Executive Summary

Our team's market research focused on the Tunisian healthy-snack and functional-food category, with particular attention to purchasing behavior, price sensitivity, and digital adoption. The available data points to a market with genuine appetite for healthier snacking, constrained by price sensitivity and still-developing e-commerce habits.

## Business Context

Tunisia's snack category is undergoing a gradual shift driven by rising fitness culture and health awareness, but this shift is not yet reflected in mainstream purchasing habits. Understanding the size and shape of this gap was essential to setting realistic growth objectives.

## Analysis

### Purchase Behavior

- A majority of Tunisian consumers rarely purchase premium healthy foods; only **16% are regular buyers**, indicating a market still in an early-adoption phase for this category.
- **Over 40% of online shoppers spend less than 50 TND/month**, reflecting a generally price-conscious online purchasing pattern that directly affects average order value expectations.
- Traditional grocers continue to dominate snack sales overall, meaning digital channels are a growth lever rather than the current primary purchase channel.

### Category Trends

- Growing demand for healthy snacks, driven by expanding gym culture and broader lifestyle awareness.
- Rising adoption of online shopping and delivery services in Tunisia, creating room for e-commerce-led growth.
- Increasing consumer interest in sustainability and natural ingredients, particularly among health-conscious segments — relevant to More Life Nutrition's plant-based positioning.

### Demographic Patterns

- Younger consumers (Gen Z) rely heavily on influencers and social proof when evaluating new products.
- Older shoppers place more weight on affordability than on brand storytelling.
- This divergence means a single, undifferentiated marketing message is unlikely to perform well across the full addressable audience.

## Key Findings

- The market opportunity is real but narrow today: regular buyers of premium healthy snacks are a minority, which reinforces the importance of customer acquisition and retention working in tandem (see `20_KPIs.md`).
- Price sensitivity is a market-wide characteristic, not a brand-specific weakness — meaning value communication matters as much as pricing itself.
- Digital and mobile-first behavior is rising, which supports a digital-led growth strategy even though overall online spend remains modest.

## Recommendations

- Set growth targets that account for a still-developing category rather than an established one (see the objective-setting logic in `12_Marketing_Objectives.md`).
- Segment messaging by demographic: social-proof-driven content for younger audiences, value/affordability-driven content for older or more price-sensitive segments.
- Treat e-commerce growth as a multi-year trend to build toward, not a channel to over-rely on for near-term volume.

> **Evidence note:** The original report includes the 16% and 40% estimates above but the supplied package does not include the underlying studies, citations, or datasets. Treat them as unverified figures quoted by the report, not independently verified or current market statistics.

> **Scope note:** Broader market-sizing data (total category value, year-over-year growth rate, number of competing brands) was not provided in the source material and is not included here.
