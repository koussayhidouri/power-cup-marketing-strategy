# 03. Business Problem

## Executive Summary

More Life Nutrition has a differentiated product and the early infrastructure of a digital presence, but has not yet translated either into strong brand awareness or sustained sales. This section defines the core business problem our team was engaged to solve.

## Business Context

Despite a strong underlying product concept and an existing e-commerce and social media presence, More Life Nutrition faces the classic challenge of an early-growth-stage consumer brand: strong potential, weak structure. The brand risks being outcompeted not on product quality, but on visibility, trust, and consistency of digital execution.

## The Core Problem

Our team's analysis converged on a single guiding strategic question:

> **How can More Life Nutrition scale its brand awareness and sales in Tunisia while maintaining a strong "healthy yet indulgent" positioning in a price-sensitive market?**

This question contains three interlinked challenges that this project addresses:

1. **Awareness** — More Life Nutrition has limited brand recognition compared to established snack and fitness brands.
2. **Positioning integrity** — Growth cannot come at the cost of diluting the "guilt-free indulgence" positioning that differentiates the brand.
3. **Price sensitivity** — Tunisia's market treats healthy snacks as a discretionary, sometimes luxury, purchase rather than a necessity, which directly affects repeat-purchase behavior.

## Analysis

The business problem is compounded by structural factors identified in our situation analysis (see `06_SWOT.md` and `11_Digital_Marketing_Audit.md`):

- **Weak digital presence**: the brand lacks a structured content, engagement, and paid media strategy.
- **Limited distribution**: heavy reliance on niche "Bio" shops and select supermarket chains restricts national accessibility.
- **Low perceived differentiation**: without clear communication, Power Cup risks being seen as "simple nuts" that could be substituted by cheaper alternatives.
- **Irregular purchase frequency**: only 16% of Tunisians are regular buyers of premium healthy foods, meaning the brand must continually acquire new customers rather than relying on a stable repeat base.

## Key Findings

- The problem is not a product problem — it is a **visibility, trust, and conversion** problem.
- Digital channels are the most viable lever for solving it, given the brand's limited budget relative to established competitors and the product's strong visual/social compatibility.
- Any solution must simultaneously build awareness *and* protect the premium-but-accessible positioning — a tension that shapes the strategic choices in `13_Marketing_Strategy.md`.

## Recommendations

- Treat brand awareness and value perception as a joint objective, not sequential ones — messaging should build recognition and justify price at the same time.
- Prioritize digital channels (SEO, social media, paid media) as the primary growth lever, given the product's natural fit with visual, story-driven content.
- Use the RACE framework (`13_Marketing_Strategy.md`) to ensure the response to this problem is structured across the full funnel, not concentrated only on top-of-funnel awareness.
